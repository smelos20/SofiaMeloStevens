Environment Artist & Sound Designer: Archeomancy

A collaborative UE5 project about an archaeologist who discovers a magic book that lets him reshape stone, built around solving puzzles to climb a ruined underground city.

EngineUnreal Engine 5
Team5 junior designers
TypeCollaborative project
Timeline9 weeks
Download playable game

My role

Environment art and sound design across the whole project.

  • Modeled the environment's rock formations, pillars, crystals, and a rock enemy
  • Solved a foliage style challenge, landing on a middle ground between realistic and stylized after research
  • Built sound from the ground up: unique footsteps per surface, distinct music per level, full intro/outro audio
  • Edited and produced the game's trailer

Story

Pekelo, an amateur archaeologist, has been waiting for a rare solar tide to enter a mysterious cave. Inside, he falls into a massive underground world: the ruins of a lost Greek city. He finds the Tome of Archeomancy, a book that turns out to be magic. Reading from it can reshape stone itself. Above, out of reach, stands the Temple of Poseidon. Now Pekelo must climb his way up through the ruins using the tome's power to reach it.

Concepting the world

We started by concepting the character. Each of us sketched a basic version of Pekelo to explore what fit the tone we wanted, and we went with Mia's concept as the best match for the game.

Early modeling

I began with the rock formations and pillars that would anchor the space, then the team built a player mockup to lay out the level and identify what needed more attention.

Design doc and player zoo

After creating the design doc, we were already working on the player zoo. We tested all the abilities we wanted to see what we needed and what should be scrapped. All of the coding was done by Mia Dunlap and Elliot Prilutskiy.

Refining the level

We started to block out a basic level for the main demo, modeling more items to fill it out. We wanted the level to feel linear while still giving players room to explore. As the layout took shape, I modeled additional pieces: stalagmites, crystals, and a rock enemy.

Before and after

Solving foliage

Foliage proved to be the biggest technical challenge. I had limited prior experience modeling it, and finding a style between realistic and stylized took real iteration.

Sound design

With the environment underway, I moved into sound: building an audio pre-vis, then implementing it in Unreal, with unique footstep sounds per surface, distinct music for each level section, and all intro/outro audio.

Final trailer

I also edited and produced the trailer for the finished game, using Unreal's video sequencer and putting the final video together in After Effects.

The vision and the final product

This project reinforced how much collaboration and balance matter on a small team, creating environment art, sound, and level design in parallel on a tight timeline.
